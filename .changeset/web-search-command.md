New `instafy web search "<query>" --json` finds links for Octo to read, with the space's own search key, in Octo's turns only. Which providers it uses, and in what order, is the web-research skill's data (`references/search-providers.json`, built into the CLI): Brave Search with `BRAVE_SEARCH_API_KEY`, then Kagi with `KAGI_API_KEY`, through one generic provider (GET or POST, the key in a header or as a Bearer token). The answer's `status` is `ok`, `not_found`, `unavailable` or `no_provider`; without a key, the first answer in a space carries the secret-request card for the key ("Everyone in this space can search with this key, up to 100 searches a day. Brave bills the key's owner."). Each result is its rank, link and site, with its title and snippet redacted inside `<untrusted_page_content>`; a result on a site whose terms bar AI agents (or a translator's, cache's or archive's copy of one) keeps only its link and the site's name, and non-web links, links with credentials and private or local addresses are dropped. A key goes only to its own provider's host and is never printed or written; nothing is cached; parameters that ask for page text (`extra_snippets`, `summary`, `extract`) are never sent. Per turn at most 4 searches (8 results by default, 20 at most), one at a time per provider, the same search never sent twice, a 429 or a refused key stops that provider for the turn, every search ends 5 s before the turn's deadline, and a space's key makes at most 100 searches a day. A query that looks like a key or a password is refused, and e-mail addresses and long numbers are removed before it is sent. Offline fixtures of test builds now also answer POST requests by their JSON body.
